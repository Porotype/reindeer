01 · Logo match

The rest pose renders through Camera.Logo. Every planar facet group votes on its colour by sampling Reindeer.png and snapping to the 13 fills in the SVG. A least-squares fit then recovers the artwork's implied light direction. While turning, each facet keeps its logo colour scaled by how its lighting changed, so the rest frame matches the artwork and motion stays believable.

Camera.Logo is close but not exact. A silhouette fit (area and centroid against the artwork) scales the frame by about 1.09 and shifts it slightly, and then the neck and head outline match. The antlers still differ from the drawing in spread and angle.

Facets hidden at rest fall back to the brand ramp. Where the model's facets don't line up with the drawing, the overlay shows the gap. That is a modelling question, not a shading one.

02 · Gaze, neck fixed

The rig already allows this. Head pivots at the jaw hinge (y 4.8), and neck vertices between y 3.6 and 4.6 are blended 10–33% to the chest, so the neck bends softly. The base stays put. Yaw is clamped to −30…65° and pitch to −25…18° relative to the chest, including the 19° artwork turn.

Glance-and-hold motion, where the head moves only when the target drifts past about 6°, reads as an animal. Continuous tracking reads as a turret. No eye geometry is needed.

03 · Voice

This is feasible and cheap. OpenAI Realtime over WebRTC gives a remote MediaStream. An AnalyserNode on it yields the envelope with less than one frame of latency, and a 40–80 ms output delay lets the jaw lead the sound slightly, the way real speech does. OpenAI sends no visemes, and transcript deltas aren't time-aligned to audio, so the motion comes from the audio itself.

A low-poly mascot doesn't need lip shapes. A 4–8° chin hinge plus prosody (nods, ear perks, a raised head while speaking) is enough to read as "the mascot is talking" without looking goofy.
